THE INPUT BEHIND THE HARVEST

Fertiliser is food policy.

Nutrients help sustain yields, but the cost is shared between farmers and the public budget. Imports, energy prices, subsidy design, uneven application and soil health all matter. Cutting use blindly risks yields; the goal is to use the right nutrient, at the right rate, in the right place.

THE LESS VISIBLE DEPENDENCY

Pulse supply can be tight.

India is a major producer and consumer of pulses, yet harvest variability and changing demand can still drive imports and price spikes. A resilient plan needs productivity, procurement, storage and dependable trade—not just a single self-sufficiency target.

HOUSEHOLD EXPOSURE

When essentials rise in price, lower-income households have less room to absorb the shock. Food inflation is also a demand shock: less money remains for transport, clothing, education and local businesses.

THE VIDEO / 4F LENS

A SUMMARY OF THE THINK SCHOOL VIDEO

Fuel. Food. Fertiliser. Finance.

The video frames India’s vulnerability as a connected “4F” system. Fuel disruption can raise import and production costs; fertiliser is both an agricultural input and a fiscal commitment; food prices affect household purchasing power; and financial volatility can weaken the rupee, making imports costlier. It invokes the 1960s food crisis as a warning about strategic dependence, then points toward resilience as both policy challenge and opportunity.

Read the claims with care. Some figures and crisis forecasts in the video are time-sensitive or were not independently verified here. This explainer uses the video as a framing source and uses official budget and food data for displayed figures.

03 / WHAT COULD CHANGE

From big ambition
to workable steps.

The utopian direction is useful as a north star. Real policy must protect farmer income, consumer affordability and yields while changing incentives over time.

01

Pay for measured efficiency

Pair soil testing and crop-specific advice with incentives for balanced nutrient use. Pilot direct, farmer-facing support or savings-sharing only where yields, access and farmer incomes are tracked.

02

Build oilseed value chains

Invest in better seed, extension, irrigation efficiency, storage and local processing. Keep trade policy predictable so farmers and processors can invest, while using targeted consumer support if prices spike.

03

Make pulses a dependable crop

Improve varieties, procurement and market access; connect pulse-growing regions to storage and processing; use imports as a transparent buffer in poor harvest years.

04

Reduce losses after harvest

Cold chains, warehouses, better market links and timely price data can make more of the harvest reach people and reduce avoidable volatility.

05

Publish a food-resilience ledger

Join budget allocations to outcomes: yield stability, import exposure, soil indicators, farmer margins, nutrition and consumer prices. Give every number a source, date and uncertainty label.

THE LONG VIEW

A food system that does not panic when the world does.

Imagine a future where farms restore soil, use nutrients precisely, draw more energy locally, and grow a broader mix of crops. Domestic oilseeds and pulses thrive where ecologically suited. Food loss is low, public procurement is smart, farmers earn fairly, and every household can afford a nutritious plate. Imports remain a choice and a buffer—not a single point of failure.

This is a direction, not a forecast. It depends on research, public investment, farmer choice, water and land constraints, infrastructure and years of transition.
